What is Ocean Accididfication?

Every day, we hear about how carbon dioxide polluting the atmosphere is causing climate change and killing our planet. Carbon pollution isn't limited to the atmosphere though. Carbon dioxide is absorbed by the ocean, too. Studies have shown that there's 50 times more carbon dioxide in the ocean than in the atmosphere...proportionally. Acidity is measured on the pH Scale, with 7 in the middle. Anything lower is acidic, and anything higher is alkaline. Right now, the ocean is actually Alkaline. Here's the justification for calling it Ocean Acidification. Carbon dioxide released into the atmosphere is absorbed by the Ocean. Through a chemical reaction, the Carbon dioxide produces a byproduct of Hydrogen Ions. This lowers the pH level of the Ocean and makes it more acidic.

Historic Acidity Levels

How do we know that the ocean is becoming more acidic? Due to the H.M.S. Challenger. The H.M.S. Challenger was a ship from the Mid 17th century that was an important milestone for the marine exploration. The H.M.S. Challenger collected an abunduce of marine samples of both the water and the actual marine life, both flora and fauna. Some of the marine life collected includes starfish, brachiopod Abyssothyris wyvilleis, and sea pigs. In the expedition, 47 hundred new species were found. Because of these samples, we can see how the ocean's acidity has changed. Scientists have discovered that the pH level has decreased from around 8.2 to around 8.05. A difference of 0.15 might not sound like much, but keep reading to see why it is.

Why it Matters

Scientists have proven that shells of marine life today are smaller than during the years of the Challenger expedition. This doesn't just apply to animals, coral has also become increasingly bleached and isn't growing as healthily. This affects a lot of reef animals. The shells of some animals like "the Butterflies of the Sea"(parakods) have decreased by more than 75%. Part of the reason is Ocean Accidification. Many animals, such as salmon and bass, depend on these too. If we don't act on this fast, the impact could be felt as high up in the food chain as us. Many seashore communities rely on seafood as a major source of protein and income. Ocean Acidification could increase poverty and destroy the livelihood of thousands.
